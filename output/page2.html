Slovakia: Katarzia – “Pozvi ma, neprídem” LP

📅 Jun 30, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Sincere and outspoken, new album from singer-songwriter Katarzia offers a variety of moods, beats and vocal techniques.

Katarzia has become an established feature of a Slovak scene even though she doesn’t live in the country anymore. Moving to Prague brought an extent of perspective and even more freedom in her lyrics.

Her power and experience also reflect in the form – she’s proved she’s not afraid to rap and adds the skill to her CV. As well as delivering some of her messages in Czech language.

Katrarzia’s eighth album “Pozvi ma, neprídem” is a collaboration across generations (with help of a pianist Robin Galia, a bassist Laura Jašková or a composer Emília Laurincová).

You don’t believe everything anyone says but if someone reveals they prefer fast food over a gym or like to sip a truck driver drink, you know they don’t give a damn about other people’s opinion, like in an opening track “Pozvi ma, neprídem”.

Minimalist beats in “Máme si čo povedať” and “Všechno mi padá” are those tranquility islands in ever changing parts throughout the whole album. Like with an electronic beat altering to techno resulting in a whispered prayer in “Žena se stáva mlhou, když se odnaučí milovat”.

“Empatia sa bude učiť ako matika” could be a reflection to Katarzias’s home country over an up to date hip hop beat. “Nechcem mačku ani psa” works as a reminder to follow your own needs and that it’s perfectly fine to be a good person caring for yourself as well as others.

Repetitiveness of a beat in “Vařit, prát, šít a rodit” mirrors the replaying stereotype of a lifestyle expected by so many but clearly not happening here in Katarzia’s case.

And these are only a couple of highlights from Katarzia’s new piece of music, as sincere and outspoken as we’re used to.

Belgium/Albania: Gala Dragot – “twerking music” LP

📅 Jun 25, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

From The Voice Kids over Eurosong to experimental dark jazzy music, it’s not your average career path. It is the road that 20-year-old Gala Dragot is on right now, though.

Born in Brussels in 2005, she is a Belgian-Albanian singer-songwriter and the daughter of artist Robert Aliaj Dragot and translator Mimoza Dino. She first appeared on a wider public radar after winning The Voice Kids Belgium in 2020, and soon after released her debut single “Contagious”, which received notable attention and airplay.

In 2023 she further established herself in Belgium’s pop landscape by competing in Eurosong, the national selection for Eurovision, with her self-written track “T’inquiète”, finishing third while receiving the highest score from the professional jury.

Since those early pop-oriented steps, Dragot has gradually moved toward a more experimental, genre-blurring identity.

She has collaborated with acts such as The Subs, worked with Zwangere Guy on the Putain soundtrack (“Equinox”), and expanded into acting with a role in Come Back. Her recent artistic profile also includes a high-profile performance for Dries Van Noten during Paris Fashion Week 2026, underlining her crossover between music, fashion, and performance art.

Her debut EP “twerking music” is presented as a stylistically eclectic project, combining piano-led compositions with elements of experimental pop, jazz-inflected harmonies, free-form structures, and glitchy electronic textures.

Across tracks such as “femcel anthem”, “bees and we”, and “punish me”, Dragot explores themes that are consistently documented in interviews and press material: digital overwhelm, identity formation, body image anxiety, emotional intensity, and the pressures of online visibility.

What makes the EP stand out is its refusal to settle into a conventional pop framework. Instead, it often prioritises atmosphere, fragmentation, and emotional expression over traditional hooks. This can make it challenging on first listen, but also more rewarding over time. There is a clear artistic ambition to move beyond her early talent-show image and into a more auteur-driven space.

The EP is not flawless in its execution — some ideas feel more like sketches than fully developed songs — but it is undeniably bold for a debut. Its strongest quality lies in its cohesion of mood: a dark, theatrical, and emotionally exposed sound world that feels intentionally unstable.

There is a strong chance that fans of Soap&Skin, Zola Jesus, Maria Arnal i Marcel Bagés, Julia Holter, or The Dresden Dolls could spend hours absorbed in “twerking music” by Gala Dragot.

Overall, “twerking music” positions Gala Dragot less as a mainstream pop export and more as a young experimental voice still defining her language. It is uneven, but compelling, and it confirms her as one of the more distinctive emerging artists in the Belgian alternative scene.

The Heartbeat of the Band

At the center of it all is Yann Gaslain, the band’s visionary singer and producer. Yann has always approached his work with a simple, yet powerful philosophy: music should be an act of nourishment. Whether he’s writing original compositions or re-imagining a classic, his focus is always on the listener’s emotional experience.

Helping anchor that vision is musical producer and pianist Rodrigo G. Pahlen. If you listen closely to the textures in “Jungle Maluca,” you can hear the touch he brings to the piano—it’s that undeniable, almost effortless magic that makes the track feel both grounded and transportive.

Why “Jungle Maluca” Hits Different

“Jungle Maluca” feels like a natural progression for Soulcare. They’ve taken their delicate, jazz-infused DNA and pushed it toward something a bit more rhythmic and adventurous. It’s the kind of song that works just as well on a sun-drenched terrace as it does through your headphones on a quiet commute.

It’s sophisticated, yes, but it’s never clinical. It stays true to the band’s central theme—a love letter to lovers—while feeling like a modern, breezy anthem for the summer.

More Than Just a Track

In an era where much of the music we hear feels manufactured, Soulcare stands out because they prioritize the feeling of the song above all else. They’re inviting you to slow down, take a breath, and celebrate love. It’s a reminder that music is one of the best tools we have for taking care of our own souls.

Soulcare – Vuelvo al Sur: A Cinematic Reimagining of Piazzolla

📅 May 11, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Returning to the Heart: Soulcare’s Haunting New Take on “Vuelvo al Sur”

There are some songs you don’t just listen to—you inhabit them. Astor Piazzolla’s “Vuelvo al Sur” has always been one of those pieces, a heavy, beautiful anthem for anyone who has ever felt the pull of home.

But with their latest single, Soulcare isn’t just treading old ground. They’ve managed to strip the classic down to its emotional bones and rebuild it into something that feels cinematic, modern, and achingly private.

A Conversation in the Dark

The magic of this version lies in the chemistry between Yann Gaslain and Johanna Zohler. Instead of a traditional “vocal performance,” their duet feels like a late-night conversation. Gaslain’s voice provides a grounded, soulful anchor, while Zohler’s delivery adds a delicate, almost ghost-like texture.

They aren’t just singing the lyrics; they are living in the space between nostalgia and hope.

The Buenos Aires Connection

You can’t fake the atmosphere of this track, and that’s likely because Soulcare went to the source. Recorded in Buenos Aires, the production carries the weight of the city.

The addition of the brilliant Hernán Jacinto on piano was a masterstroke. His playing sidesteps the clichés of traditional tango, weaving in jazz influences that make the arrangement feel fluid and alive. It’s rich, textured, and sophisticated without ever feeling over-produced.

Why You Need to Listen

In a world of high-tempo, “look-at-me” pop, “Vuelvo al Sur” is a rare moment of stillness. It’s a song for the blue hours—the late nights or early mornings when you’re reflecting on where you’ve been and where you’re headed.

Soulcare has given us an elegant, melancholic bridge between the golden age of tango and the modern world. It’s more than a cover; it’s a tribute to the South, and a stunning piece of “soul care” for the rest of us.

It’s easy for modern pop to feel a bit “produced by a committee,” but every so often, you stumble onto something that feels like it was built by hand. That’s the immediate vibe of “Spanish Sunset,” the latest single from Anderson Rose.

Based out of the Austin area—a place that knows a thing or two about good songwriting—Anderson Rose isn’t your typical indie outfit. It’s the creative engine of John Anderson and Ron Rose, two guys who have spent years behind the scenes and in the studio mastering the art of the “perfect take.”

More Than Just a Pop Song

“Spanish Sunset” isn’t trying to be a viral TikTok snippet. It’s a wide-screen, cinematic piece of pop that actually breathes.

While the duo is known for bouncing between genres—one day they’re leaning into rock, the next it’s something more atmospheric—this track captures a specific kind of “golden hour” energy. You can hear the Austin influence in the precision of the instrumentation, but the soul of the track is purely international.

What makes the Anderson Rose sound work?

  • The Chemistry: John Anderson is a multi-instrumentalist who has been obsessed with the craft since he was ten. He handles the technical heavy lifting, from drums to bass to production.

  • The Vision: Ron Rose brings a unique perspective to the table. Having worked with legends like Oscar Peterson and members of Daft Punk on the industry side, he knows what a “master-crafted” song sounds like from the inside out.

  • The Polish: Unlike a lot of home-studio projects, Anderson Rose tracks have a high-fidelity shimmer. It sounds like a million bucks without losing its heart.

From the Texas Hill Country to the World

Austin might be the “Live Music Capital of the World,” but John and Ron aren’t staying in one lane. Their mission is to create music that “traverses a broad landscape.”

“Spanish Sunset” feels like the centerpiece of that mission. It’s got the melodic hooks that stick in your head for days, but it’s layered with the kind of sophisticated production that reveals something new every time you listen. It’s the kind of track that fits just as well on a long drive as it does on a high-end stereo at home.

A Vibrant Fusion of African and Latin Rhythms, Folk, Jazz, and Soulful Storytelling

Capeverdean-born singer-songwriter Cintia Maio makes a stunning debut with her single, “Dance To The Sun,” a song that encapsulates joy, rhythm, and the boundless power of following one’s dreams. Seamlessly blending African and Latin rhythms, folk music, jazz, and global influences, Maio’s music is an evocative experience that resonates with authenticity, spirituality, and deep emotional connection.

A Journey of Resilience, Purpose, and Musical Awakening

Born and raised in Mindelo, Cape Verde, a coastal city known for its rich musical traditions and vibrant artistic culture, Cintia Maio grew up in a household where music was more than just a form of entertainment—it was a way of life. From an early age, she was drawn to songwriting and singing, effortlessly weaving melodies that reflected her emotions and experiences. However, societal expectations and personal responsibilities led her to put her artistic aspirations on hold.

For years, Maio dedicated herself to community service, finding fulfillment in helping others. Yet, deep inside, she felt a longing—a calling she could no longer ignore. At the age of 43, as a mother of two teenagers, she made the bold decision to pursue the path she was always meant to walk. “Dance To The Sun” is the manifestation of that leap of faith, a song that embodies courage, renewal, and the unwavering belief that it is never too late to follow one’s true purpose.

The Soul-Stirring Essence of “Dance To The Sun”

More than just a song, “Dance To The Sun” is a rhythmic celebration of life and self-discovery. Infused with upbeat Latin percussion, hypnotic melodies, and an irresistible groove, the track invites listeners to move freely, embrace their inner light, and surrender to the joy of the present moment.

“Music has always been inside me, guiding me, even when I wasn’t ready to listen,” says Maio. “This song is a declaration that it’s never too late to follow your purpose. I hope it inspires others to trust their inner voice, embrace life’s rhythms, and step into the light of their dreams.”

A Spiritual and Emotional Soundscape

The essence of “Dance To The Sun” lies in its ability to transport listeners to a place of pure joy and empowerment. Each note and lyric carries the weight of Maio’s personal journey, making the track not just an invitation to dance, but a call to self-discovery, healing, and faith. Drawing inspiration from her Capeverdean roots and global musical influences, she blends rich textures and organic instrumentation to create a sound that is as deeply spiritual as it is electrifying.

Beyond its infectious beats, “Dance To The Sun” is a powerful message of resilience. Maio’s journey of stepping into her purpose serves as a beacon for those who have ever doubted their own dreams. She reminds us all that no matter where we are in life, it is never too late to rewrite our story and move to the rhythm of our own destiny.

Where to Listen

“Dance To The Sun” is now available on all major streaming platforms, bringing its uplifting energy to a global audience. Whether you’re looking for a song to dance to, a melody to inspire you, or a rhythm that speaks to your soul, Cintia Maio’s debut single delivers an experience that lingers long after the music fades.

G-Deep (Gagandeep Narain) and Uzzi Malik (Usman Malik) are dynamic artists hailing from India and Pakistan, respectively, who have joined forces to create a groundbreaking musical collaboration. G-Deep is an Indian-Punjabi singer and songwriter renowned for his innovative fusion of traditional Punjabi music with modern genres like hip-hop and R&B. His work, characterized by a unique blend of cultural authenticity and contemporary sound, has played a significant role in bringing Punjabi music to a global audience.

Aaa Ni Kuriye’s lyrics reflect a mix of romantic teasing and a confident, almost boastful vibe. The repeated lines, “Come here or go away Girl,” hint at playful tension, with the singer inviting the girl to approach but also implying that he’s fine either way, showing a self-assured attitude.

The song goes on to emphasize themes of success and appeal, with references to fame (“His songs are hit now,” “Now my name ringing all over”) and wealth (“Twenty Bands on me,” “First class ticket to Dubai”). The singer portrays himself as someone who has “made it” in life and is no longer interested in those who can’t keep up, adding, “Broke boys stay in ya lane.” There’s an allure of living an exciting lifestyle in the “crazy city” of New York, and he positions himself as a “bandit” type, charismatic and edgy.

Additional lines like “Your man is like opium” suggest that he sees himself as addictive or irresistible, admired widely, with a persona that’s hard to replicate. This combination of allure, confidence, and romantic tension gives the song an intriguing mix of flirtation and swagger.

“Desi Munde” is an electrifying music project that brings together the phenomenal talents of G-Deep from India and Uzzi Malik from Pakistan. This groundbreaking collaboration showcases unity through a mutual love of music, seamlessly blending modern and traditional sounds from both regions. With a strong emphasis on cross-cultural fusion, “Desi Munde” captures the spirit of global music trends while honoring the rich musical traditions of South Asia.
