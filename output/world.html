Norway: Andreas Rotevatn – “Mellom saltvatn og sola” LP

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Norwegian trombonist, singer and composer Andreas Rotevatn, together with his band, offers us a dazzling prog-pop-jazz-dance symphony.

Andreas Rotevatn launched his solo career two years ago with the album “Av daude gror”, and now we have a follow-up. Titled “Mellom saltvatn og sola” (“Between Salt Water and the Sun”), with a reference to the myth of Icarus, it is magnificent and intense while remaining wholeheartedly entertaining.

Yes, Rotevatn is unpredictable and daring, which shouldn’t surprise anyone familiar with his jazz background. Yet at the same time, you could dance to almost every song on this album, and you’ll find yourself trying to sing along despite the fact that all the lyrics are in Norwegian.

Besides Rotevatn’s band – Nikolai Hængsle on bass, Sander Nordahl on guitar and Elias Tafjord on drums – the album features as many as ten guest appearances, including Lindstrøm, Side Brok and Tuva Syvertsen, who sings on three tracks that are among the record’s highlights.

If, with “Mellom saltvatn og sola”, Rotevatn wanted to show that even the most unexpected compromises are possible, he has more than succeeded. But apparently, he wasn’t thinking only about music.

Sudan: Sulaf – “ABA” (ابّ ا.) LP

📅 Oct 22, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Nubian tradition meets contemporary production and memories of old Sudan on a magnificent new album by Sulaf.

“How do we carry yesterday’s Sudan into tomorrow? What stories do we pass on to our children, and to the world? How do we keep these meanings alive when we are all scattered everywhere?”

These are the questions Sudanese singer, composer and oud player Sulaf asked herself while working on her splendid new album “ABA” (ابّ ا.), which is also an homage to the Sufi poet and musician Aba, her grandfather.

It’s justified to say that on “ABA”, Sulaf has created – or recreated – a whole world, where the memories of her grandfather are placed in the context of memories of her beloved country that she had to leave.

And she forms these personal stories into magnificent songs that draw from Nubian tradition yet don’t stray from contemporary production – she worked in the studio with producer Maxime Kosinetz, known for similarly old-but-modern works by Imarhan and Tinariwen.

“These songs have always been my naffag, a small window into Aba’s world. Through them, I return to my Sudan,” Sulaf writes in her comments on the album. “What binds these songs together is love, the timeless love that brought Eve to Adam, and that keeps Aba alive, now and forever.”

Faradjallah & Attarazat Addahabia – Al Hadaoui

📅 Oct 13, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

During the ’50s, Faradjallah was booked as a singer for surprise parties with friends. He started to write his first songs including “L’gnawi” in 1967 and wanted to make people discover Gnawa culture, or maybe rather his take on the culture to be more exact. Faradjallah recalls his first interaction with the genre in the streets of the Dern neighbourhood, where he used to go to elementary school.

Al Hadaoui Al Hadaoui

Gnawa is one of the essential musical genres of Morocco. It combines ritual poetry with traditional dances and music linked with a spiritual foundation. Musically a lot of influences originated from West Africa as well as Sudan. Gnawa is usually played by a selection of specific instruments such as the qaraqab (large iron castanets centrally associated with the music), the hajhouj (a three string lute), guembri loudaâ (a three stringed bass instrument) and the tbel (large drums). People would put shells on their clothes and instruments and use incense at their parties. “Sidi darbo lalla – lala derbo khadem…” came from Gnawa verses Faradjallah used to sing when he was 14. The lyrics tackle a global (im)balance of power and the question of social status in this course.

The band Attarazat Addahabia was formed in 1968. The original line-up included 14 members, all from the same family. They played their first small concerts here and there starting in 1969. Later in 1973 they performed bigger shows for instance at the Municipal Theatre followed by the “Al Massira Show” at Velodrome Stadium in downtown Casablanca. Their first album “Al Hadaoui” (the one you are listening to) was recorded at Boussiphone studios in 1972 and was never released before. Nobody seems to remember the exact reason why Boussiphone ended up deci- ding not to put the album out. The album’s title track also served as the basis for Fadoul’s “Mak- toub Lah”, who frequented the same circles as the band for some time.
Their shows sometimes could go as long as 12 hours, starting at 5pm in the afternoon, with an occasional break here and there. In the 1980s the band took a brief break. Faradjallah recalled the reason for that break like this: “Zaki, the bands drummer, had fallen in love with a young girl from Mohammedia. Soon after, he fell very ill. The group members were convinced that the girl had given him ‘s’hor’ (a kind of local Moroccan version of “black magic”). For four years, the whole group stopped playing. It was unthinkable to find another drummer to replace Zaki, even temporarily.” So they waited four years for Zaki to “get back on his feet” before going back on stage.

Belgium: Jennifur – “MAZDA” LP

📅 Oct 06, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Belgian producer Jennifur seamlessly bridges ambient electronica, UK bass rhythms, indie rock textures, and touches of classical arrangement into an almost visual journey.

With “MAZDA”, released on Off Corner Records, Belgian producer Jennifur delivers a third studio album that feels more like a wild, endless rollercoaster than a quiet drive down a deserted highway.

Engineered and produced entirely in his home studio, this 12-track project is a largely instrumental, surreal highway labyrinth. It seamlessly bridges ambient electronica, UK bass rhythms, indie rock textures, and touches of classical arrangement into an almost visual journey.

What makes the record so compelling is its constant shift in dynamics and mood, solidifying a signature sound balanced between heart-focused melody and precise synthesis.

Jennifur effortlessly switches from dreamy passages and bouncy, energetic rhythms to nervous, jittery interplay, only to plunge just as quickly into desperate flailing or raw frenzy.

That is how the captivating “You Always Knew That Feeling” (featuring vocals by Lindy Versyck from TJE, who were recently featured on beehype) glitches you relentlessly across a bumpy road from one side of the car to the other — the backseat is definitely not the best place to be sitting here.

Besides this, the trip also offers much-needed pit stops to catch your breath. With “In The Sun”, it’s smooth sailing toward the coast with the window rolled down and the wind in your hair.

Meanwhile, the almost radio-friendly “Need You By My Side” (featuring Dutch singer-songwriter TEUN) provides a familiar landmark—two singles that served as the trigger for many to explore this record after climbing into the Belgian PlayRight Top 100. Along the way, subtle Madchester-revival vibes and quiet intermezzos give you the chance to pull over for a moment and stretch your legs.

It isn’t until the closing track, “Dancin’ Around”, that Jennifur opens the parachute for a safe arrival home, calling back to the home front to say the soup can be put on the stove.

MAZDA is not an album for those who easily get dizzy from a bit of chaos and hectic energy. However, it is an absolute must-listen for anyone wondering what it feels like when late-night highway reflection meets a wild amusement park ride. Fasten your seatbelt — don’t say we didn’t warn you.

Türkiye/Denmark: Hilal Kaya – “Sümbül”

📅 Sep 23, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Deeply rooted in Turkish folk music, Hilal Kaya beautifully blends it with jazz, rock, and psychedelic elements.

Let me introduce you to Hilal Kaya. She is a Turkish-Danish vocalist, songwriter, and composer who just released her debut album, “Sümbül”, via CPL Music / Nordic Notes.

What I love about this album is the close connection I feel to the ’70s Turkish psychedelic folk rock era, a movement that was not only famous in Türkiye but also created a huge wave around the globe. “Sümbül” is deeply rooted in Turkish folk music, beautifully blending it with jazz, rock, and psychedelic elements.

Alongside Hilal, a stellar group of musicians brings this album to life. We hear Søren Bigum on guitars and keys with his distinct playing style. Orhan Özgür Turan plays the oud and bağlama, bringing the microtonal world of Anatolia to your ears. When we look at the album’s groovy foundation, we see Henrik Poulsen on bass and Søren Poulsen on rhythm.

The album also includes three Turkish folk covers that reflect the distinct regions and rich heritage of Anatolia.

As a final note, the track “Sultan” (released as a single last year) was nominated for Song of the Year at the Danish Music Awards Roots 2025, while Hilal herself received a nomination in the New Roots Artist of the Year category.

Hope you enjoy this musical journey from Asia Minor to Northern Europe.

Japan: CHO CO PA CO CHO CO QUIN QUIN – “Synthetic 海岸” LP

📅 Sep 09, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Tropical textures, polyrhythms, vintage Latin-Caribbean percussions, synthesizers, and everyday field recordings – CHO CO PA CO CHO CO QUIN QUIN create music that is bright, playful, and deeply physical.

Three years after their debut album, “tradition”, they return with their second release called “Synthetic 海岸” (“Synthetic Coast”).

Drawing on sounds associated with Latin America, India, Africa, and Japan, the trio uses electronic sounds, percussion, and field recordings to reconstruct memories of different places and create an imaginary coast.

The members’ backgrounds are central to this approach. Daido studied conga at the University of Havana in Cuba, and the band’s name comes from a vocalization of the conga rhythm. Yuta, Haruomi Hosono’s grandson, became interested in electronic music as a teenager. He plays bass and 三線（sanshin). Daido and he handle composition and arrangement, while So is responsible for mixing and mastering, including recordings made on streets and inside cars.

The connection to Hosono is clear. From the imaginary tropics of Tropical Dandy and 泰安洋行 (Bon Voyage co.) to his electronic works with YMO, Hosono has repeatedly created distant places through sounds made in Japan. Their first album “tradition” also reflected this interest in tropical sounds and electronic music.

But the trio has developed its own method. They combine earthy percussion with electronics, not simply replacing traditional instruments with machines, but using electronic sound to invent a new sense of place. This is the idea behind “Synthetic 海岸”. Synthesized birds, whales, waves, and wind do not reproduce nature; they create a coast that exists only in the music.

Some songs begin with real places. “アダンの海辺” (“Adan no Umibe”) draws on the group’s 2023 visit to Amami Ōshima and paintings by 田中一村 (Ichimura Tanaka), transforming these experiences through sampling, memory, and electronic production.

The result is a kind of folk music without a single homeland: congas from Cuba, Okinawan and Amami sounds, Latin music, Tokyo street recordings, and music made inside cars are brought together into something strangely familiar.

If “tradition” introduced their musical roots, “Synthetic 海岸” expands them into an imaginary landscape. With tropical textures, polyrhythms, vintage Latin-Caribbean percussion, synthesizers, and everyday field recordings, CHO CO PA CO CHO CO QUIN QUIN create music that is bright, playful, and deeply physical—while inviting us to imagine a place that does not yet exist.

New Zealand: Sheep, Dog & Wolf – “Ceiling Waves” LP

📅 Aug 18, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

“Ceiling Waves” trades in intricate compositional arrangements and unashamed emotional depth. An album for the good times, the bad, and everything in between.

Daniel McBride aka Sheep, Dog & Wolf is a New Zealand multi-instrumentalist, vocalist, and composer who lives and works in London.

In August this year, McBride unveiled Sheep, Dog & Wolf’s third album “Ceiling Waves”. It’s a fitting title for a set of songs that rise and fall, often moving from maximal to minimal within a single movement. When beehype’s founder Mariusz Herma enthusiastically emailed about the album, he evoked the art-rock and progressive folk figures Robert Wyatt, David Sylvian, and Joanna Newsom as reference points – comparisons which all, perhaps unsurprisingly, hold water.

Released through Nacre Recordings, “Ceiling Waves” trades in intricate compositional arrangements and unashamed emotional depth. Considered within the broader context of Sheep, Dog & Wolf’s catalogue, it feels like a glorious culmination of McBride’s drive to integrate cerebral jazz, minimalism, and math-rock impulses with the visceral immediacy of McBride’s id.

There’s a lot of grief in these songs, but I could never reduce the music or songwriting down to one feeling alone. No, this is work about the full spectrum of life while you’re living with the pain of loss, one day at a time.

On “Guessing”, delicate psychedelic folk sensibilities collide with the hauntological ghosts of early dubstep and spluttering kick drums that recall Chicago footwork.

Later on, “We Sang As You Slept”, Stereolab-slanted keyboard figures and woozy bass stabs serve as the backdrop for a vocal sung like a paean to a higher force, occasionally collapsing into stargazed twinkles. Like the seasons and climates, many moods are on display here.

Ultimately, McBride seems to balance two forces: elegantly developed, polished melodies and arrangements, and considered, confessional lyrics penned by an artist trying to get on with it, while still feeling winded at unexpected moments. Even on the saddest days of our lives, we still sometimes have moments in the sun. By acknowledging this, “Ceiling Waves” is an absolute gift. It’s an album for the good times, the bad, and everything in between.

Argentina: Juana Sallies – “Ancora”

📅 Aug 03, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Blending orchestral arrangements with subtle electronic beats, Argentinian musician Juana Sallies evokes the depths of the ocean.

Juana Sallies is a composer, vocalist, and visual artist originally from Ushuaia — the world’s southernmost city — now based in Buenos Aires.

This year she released “Ancora”, her third solo album (in addition to her work with Turba Par and JS JS). As its title suggests, the record revolves around the image of the anchor: the object cast to the seabed to keep a vessel in place. Throughout the album, sinking becomes a way of staying grounded: a form of emotional resilience, as Sallies herself describes it.

Released via Isla Desierta Discos, “Ancora” features nine songs that evoke the depths of the ocean through a rich sonic palette, blending orchestral arrangements with subtle electronic beats. Sallies’ deep sensitivity to nature and the environment allows the album to move effortlessly between contemplation, introspection, and the growing urgency of a planet under increasing ecological strain.

In that sense, the sea becomes more than a setting: it emerges as a metaphor for human existence itself, where fragility, resilience, and the search for grounding coexist beneath the surface.

Sincere and outspoken, new album from singer-songwriter Katarzia offers a variety of moods, beats and vocal techniques.

Katarzia has become an established feature of a Slovak scene even though she doesn’t live in the country anymore. Moving to Prague brought an extent of perspective and even more freedom in her lyrics.

Her power and experience also reflect in the form – she’s proved she’s not afraid to rap and adds the skill to her CV. As well as delivering some of her messages in Czech language.

Katrarzia’s eighth album “Pozvi ma, neprídem” is a collaboration across generations (with help of a pianist Robin Galia, a bassist Laura Jašková or a composer Emília Laurincová).

Minimalist beats in “Máme si čo povedať” and “Všechno mi padá” are those tranquility islands in ever changing parts throughout the whole album. Like with an electronic beat altering to techno resulting in a whispered prayer in “Žena se stáva mlhou, když se odnaučí milovat”.

“Empatia sa bude učiť ako matika” could be a reflection to Katarzias’s home country over an up to date hip hop beat. “Nechcem mačku ani psa” works as a reminder to follow your own needs and that it’s perfectly fine to be a good person caring for yourself as well as others.

Repetitiveness of a beat in “Vařit, prát, šít a rodit” mirrors the replaying stereotype of a lifestyle expected by so many but clearly not happening here in Katarzia’s case.

And these are only a couple of highlights from Katarzia’s new piece of music, as sincere and outspoken as we’re used to.

Soulcare – Vuelvo al Sur: A Cinematic Reimagining of Piazzolla

📅 May 11, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Returning to the Heart: Soulcare’s Haunting New Take on “Vuelvo al Sur”

There are some songs you don’t just listen to—you inhabit them. Astor Piazzolla’s “Vuelvo al Sur” has always been one of those pieces, a heavy, beautiful anthem for anyone who has ever felt the pull of home.

But with their latest single, Soulcare isn’t just treading old ground. They’ve managed to strip the classic down to its emotional bones and rebuild it into something that feels cinematic, modern, and achingly private.

A Conversation in the Dark

The magic of this version lies in the chemistry between Yann Gaslain and Johanna Zohler. Instead of a traditional “vocal performance,” their duet feels like a late-night conversation. Gaslain’s voice provides a grounded, soulful anchor, while Zohler’s delivery adds a delicate, almost ghost-like texture.

They aren’t just singing the lyrics; they are living in the space between nostalgia and hope.

The Buenos Aires Connection

You can’t fake the atmosphere of this track, and that’s likely because Soulcare went to the source. Recorded in Buenos Aires, the production carries the weight of the city.

The addition of the brilliant Hernán Jacinto on piano was a masterstroke. His playing sidesteps the clichés of traditional tango, weaving in jazz influences that make the arrangement feel fluid and alive. It’s rich, textured, and sophisticated without ever feeling over-produced.

Why You Need to Listen

In a world of high-tempo, “look-at-me” pop, “Vuelvo al Sur” is a rare moment of stillness. It’s a song for the blue hours—the late nights or early mornings when you’re reflecting on where you’ve been and where you’re headed.

Soulcare has given us an elegant, melancholic bridge between the golden age of tango and the modern world. It’s more than a cover; it’s a tribute to the South, and a stunning piece of “soul care” for the rest of us.

G-Deep & Uzzi Malik – Aaa Ni Kuriye ( Desi Munde EP )

📅 Nov 04, 2024  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

ArtistRack brings to you ‘Aaa Ni Kuriye’ by G-Deep & Uzzi Malik ( Desi Munde EP )

G-Deep (Gagandeep Narain) and Uzzi Malik (Usman Malik) are dynamic artists hailing from India and Pakistan, respectively, who have joined forces to create a groundbreaking musical collaboration. G-Deep is an Indian-Punjabi singer and songwriter renowned for his innovative fusion of traditional Punjabi music with modern genres like hip-hop and R&B. His work, characterized by a unique blend of cultural authenticity and contemporary sound, has played a significant role in bringing Punjabi music to a global audience.

Aaa Ni Kuriye’s lyrics reflect a mix of romantic teasing and a confident, almost boastful vibe. The repeated lines, “Come here or go away Girl,” hint at playful tension, with the singer inviting the girl to approach but also implying that he’s fine either way, showing a self-assured attitude.

The song goes on to emphasize themes of success and appeal, with references to fame (“His songs are hit now,” “Now my name ringing all over”) and wealth (“Twenty Bands on me,” “First class ticket to Dubai”). The singer portrays himself as someone who has “made it” in life and is no longer interested in those who can’t keep up, adding, “Broke boys stay in ya lane.” There’s an allure of living an exciting lifestyle in the “crazy city” of New York, and he positions himself as a “bandit” type, charismatic and edgy.

DAY 2 IN MOSHI, I GET SHOCKED

📅 Nov 17, 2023  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

On Friday 20th October 2023 I woke up feeling very refreshed, at about 9 am, I got a call from Mister Mshana my host in Moshi, he was checking up on me, and we agreed to meet at a tea shop that was very near the lodge where I was staying. The tea shop was a very nice place owned by an Indian couple, who made different kinds of tasty bites that were perfect for breakfast.

At ten we met at the tea shop and discussed our timetable for the day, Mshana had arranged that we would meet a 70 years old guitar player from the 60s who surely would have a lot to tell us on the music scene in Moshi during his younger days. Also this morning we had planned to meet Kaluu who was the member of Orchestra Zaire Success, the one I had planned to give the photo of him with his old band. It was at this time that Mshana gave me shocking information; Kaluu had died five years ago!!!

I had really counted on meeting Kaluu, who I knew had long stopped playing music but had information that he now had a small workshop where he repaired electronic gadgets. The news that he was no more, and he had infact departed five years ago was so sad and so disappointing. Penaa, one of Kaluu’s son had inherited his father’s profession and was running his father’s workshop, so we decided to visit him. We got to the workshop and were told he had gone out to look for spares. And we decided to got to the second of Mshana’s contacts, this was the 70 years old guitarist. When we arrived at our meeting point, we realized that the old man was now selling traditional medicine by the side of the road, he was busy and he told us to wait for him while he attended his clients, for sure he was quite popular because it seemed like the stream of customers just didn’t seem to end. We decide to go back to the workshop and meet Penaa, we would get back to the guitarist afterwards. Penna was back in his  workshop and he was a very friendly and welcoming guy. He confirmed to us that his father had died 5 years ago. I handed him the photo that I had thought I would hand it over to his father, Penna recognized his father, and promised to give the photo to his mother. I promised that I would try to meet his mother and get to know more about the people in the photograph. We went back to the guitarist, he wasn’t there he had closed shop.

Orchestra Zaire Success - Kaluu standing second from right
It is claimed that Orchestra Zaire Success, Orchestra Super Veya and Orchestra Fauvette, came into Tanzania from Congo at around the same time. Zaire Success settled in Moshi, Super Veya Settled in Mwanza, and Orchestra Fauvette put its base in Dar es Salaam, but thats another story.

Mr Mshana had arranged for me to meet another old man whose name was Pius, we were to meet at the Uhuru Park. Mr Pius was another very interesting person, he loved reading books. He would get a book and find a quite seat at the Uhuru Park and enjoy the book in the open fresh air from the Kilmanjaro Mountain. We  talked about books that we had both read and those we wanted to read. In our conversations, Pius told me that in his younger days he wanted to be a guitarist, but his parents were completely against that, so he ended up just being a music lover. Pius asked me if I had a copy of Salum Abdallah's ' Happy New Year song, luckily a few days ago, I found a copy  and the next time I go to Moshi,   I will make sure I give him a copy.
Salum  Abdallah started his first band in 1942 and called it La Paloma. He loved Cuban music so much that he ran away from his father's home in Morogoro and travelled to Mombasa, planning to board a ship to Cuba. Unfortunately it was in the middle of the second world war, and he ended up getting stranded in Mombasa, his father had to travel from Morogoro to recue him. Two years later in 1944 he started the Cuban Marimba Band, a band which went on to be one of the top bands in Tanzania until its collapse in the 70s. Salum Abdalla himself died in a tragic accident in November 1965.
One interesting thing that Pius mentioned was that he knew the original composer of the famous song Malaika.

Fadhili Williams is officially recognized as the composer of Malaika, but many elders in Moshi challenge that and claim that a mechanic known as Salim Adam, composed the song for his beautiful girlfriend whom he never got to marry as she went on to be married to a rich Asian.
I agreed with Pius that I would get back to him as he had promised he would even show me the house that this mechanic once lived when he was alive.

By this time it was late afternoon and we agreed to call it a day, Mshana and I  went back to the tea shop for a cup of tea. One of the clients that we met there was the owner of one of the FM radio stations in Moshi, we made an appoint for me to participate in a oldies radio program that was aired every Sunday at ten  in the morning. Mshana invited me to his home the next day promising me surprises….. I went back to my lodging and began documenting all that had passed in the last 8 hours, and wondering what surprises will Mshana have for me?
